SLIDE 1 — Title

My name is Suyog Shrestha from Knox College. Today I will be presenting 
on SDG 7 — Affordable and Clean Energy — and on what happened when I 
looked underneath a regional average."

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SLIDE 2 — Research Question                                  
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

"This is the same question I asked last time. Since 2015, electricity
access has expanded rapidly across the developing world. Has clean
cooking kept pace — and where is the gap widest and still growing?

In Presentation 1 I answered it at the level of world regions. Today I
want to know whether a regional answer can be trusted at all."

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SLIDE 3 — Recap of Presentation 1                            
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

"A quick recap. The measure is the cooking gap — electricity access
minus clean cooking access, in percentage points.

Globally that gap narrowed from 23 points to 17. Sub-Saharan Africa was
the only region both widest and still widening.

But one region stood out for the opposite reason. Central and Southern
Asia was the second-fastest narrowing region in the world — minus 12.7
points. That is the claim I am testing today."

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SLIDE 4 — Finding 1 of 3                                     
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

"So I went one level down, into the nine countries the UN puts inside
Southern Asia.

Four of the nine are moving the wrong way. Bangladesh's gap grew by
fourteen and a half points. Afghanistan by 14.3. Nepal by almost five.
Sri Lanka by almost three.

Five of the countries narrowed — India and Bhutan most of all.

The region is not moving in one direction. It is moving in two."

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SLIDE 5 — Finding 1 of 3, The Reason                         
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

"So why did the region look like a success?

Both lines on this chart cover the same nine countries. The blue line is
the UN's figure. It is population-weighted — every person counts once —
so India's 1.4 billion people set it almost single-handedly. It falls
twelve points.

The orange line counts every country once instead. It moves by zero.

India gained 32 points of clean cooking in nine years. That is a real
achievement, and it is the entire regional trend. Average the countries
instead of the people, and the improvement disappears."

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SLIDE 6 — Finding 2 of 3                                     
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

"Second finding. If the deficit is not spread evenly across countries,
is it spread evenly inside them?

It is not. The blue bars are the distance between town and village on
electricity. In six of the nine countries it is under two points — in
Bangladesh it is actually negative, meaning rural access is marginally
ahead. Electrification reached the villages.

The orange bars are the same distance for clean cooking. Sixty-four
points in Afghanistan. Sixty-one in Pakistan. Fifty-five in Bangladesh.
Pakistan's cooking divide is fourteen times its electricity divide.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SLIDE 7 — Finding 3 of 3                                     
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

"Third finding — and this one surprised me.

SDG 7 has a second target: the renewable share of a country's energy.
Grouped by that share, all 192 countries show the same pattern — clean
cooking access falls as renewables rise. Ninety-two percent in the least
renewable group, down to 17 percent in the most.

Indicator 7.2.1 counts firewood, charcoal and dung as renewable energy. 
So a country can score well on SDG 7.2 precisely because its people are 
still cooking on wood.

Sri Lanka is the sharpest case here — 55 percent renewable,
universal electricity, and clean cooking stuck at 31 percent with no net
gain since 2018.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SLIDE 8 — Emerging Conclusions                               
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

"To close. Three things.

The regional number was one country — the average of the nine moved by
zero.

Electricity is effectively finished in Southern Asia. Clean cooking is
not, and what remains of it is rural.

And one SDG 7 indicator is masking another.

One last number. On the 2015-to-2024 pace, only three of these nine
countries reach universal clean cooking by 2030 — India, Bhutan and the
Maldives. Bangladesh lands near 37 percent. Sri Lanka near 33.
