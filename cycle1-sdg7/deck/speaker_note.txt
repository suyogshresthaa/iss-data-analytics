────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SLIDE 1 — Title                                                      
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

"My name is Suyog Shrestha from Knox College. Today I will be presenting 
on SDG 7 — Affordable and Clean Energy — and a question about whether 
clean cooking has kept pace with electricity."

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SLIDE 2 — Research Question                                          
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

"Since 2015, electricity access has expanded rapidly across the developing
world. My question was whether clean cooking kept pace — and where the gap
is widest and still growing.

I'll be honest that this is not the question I started with. I began by
assuming electrification was outrunning clean cooking everywhere. The data
disproved that. So I rewrote the question rather than the finding."

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SLIDE 3 — SDG 7                                                      
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

"SDG 7 has two access targets. 7.1.1 is the share of people with
electricity. 7.1.2 is the share who cook with clean fuels.

Subtracting one from the other gives what I call the cooking gap, measured
in percentage points. A country at 97 percent electricity and 10 percent
clean cooking has a gap of 87 points. The measure asks one simple thing:
did the two forms of energy access arrive together?"

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SLIDE 4 — Where the Data Comes From                                  
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

"Everything comes from one place — the UN SDG Global Indicators Database,
pulled through its version 5 API. The underlying providers are the World
Bank for electricity and the World Health Organization for clean cooking.

2015 to 2024. 228 countries. 16,216 rows after cleaning. Python for the
extraction and cleaning, Excel for the deliverable workbook, Tableau for
the visuals.

One caveat worth mentioning is that the clean cooking figures are modelled
estimates with uncertainty bounds, not direct measurements. And 34 mostly
small territories report electricity but not clean cooking, so they have no
gap at all."

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SLIDE 5 — Finding 1 of 5                                             
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

"Globally, the answer is yes. Clean cooking rose 11 points since 2015.
Electricity rose 4.9. The gap narrowed from 23 points to 17.

Electricity looks slow only because wealthy regions are already near 100
percent and have nowhere left to climb."

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SLIDE 6 — Finding 2 of 5                                             
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

"But that global average hides a reversal. Four regions narrowed their gap.
Three widened it.

Sub-Saharan Africa is the only region that is both the widest and still
widening — 32.7 points, up almost 8 since 2015. It is moving in the
opposite direction to the rest of the world."

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SLIDE 7 — Finding 3 of 5                                             
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

"So where is the gap widest right now? Laos. 97 percent of people have
electricity. 10 percent cook with clean fuels. An 86-point gap.

Comoros follows at 80.7, Timor-Leste at 75.3. The widest gaps cluster in
Asia and the Pacific."

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SLIDE 8 — Finding 4 of 5                                             
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

"But widest and widening are different questions, and they name different
countries. Only three of fifteen appear on both lists.

Rwanda's gap grew 41 points in nine years. Twelve of the fifteen
fastest-widening countries are in Sub-Saharan Africa.

Had I asked only where the gap is widest, this would have been a
presentation about Laos — and I would have missed the reversal entirely."

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
SLIDE 9 — Finding 5 of 5                                             
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

"Finally, the national average hides the problem inside countries too.
Zimbabwe reports 37 percent nationally, which looks unremarkable in a
ranking. That is 80 percent in the cities and 8 percent in the countryside.

The deficit lives inside countries, not only between them. Electrification
has been the easier win. Clean cooking is the one still being lost — and it
is being lost in rural Sub-Saharan Africa."
